# SandBase Jev Trader — Product Spec

## One line
A live dashboard showing a model make the buy or sell call on a simulated
Anthropic order book, every 300 ms tick.

## What it is
A single-page web app. A model watches a synthetic ANTH/USD level-2 book, and
every tick answers one question: buy or sell. Every tick it posts one post-only
limit order on that side, one tick inside the touch, replacing the last one. It
fills when a taker hits it, so the desk earns the spread rather than paying it.
The page shows this happening live.

The instrument is fictional. Anthropic is private, so there is no real book to
read: the market is generated by a seeded simulation, and the dashboard says so.

## Who it is for
1. People who scroll past trading bots and stop for one that visibly acts several
   times a second. They have three seconds to get it.
2. People who click through and watch for five minutes. They should be able to
   verify everything: the decision, the order, the fill, the cost of being wrong.

## The message
Primary: "The model makes the buy or sell call itself, every 300 ms tick."
Secondary: "The market is fake. The execution path is not."

Nothing on screen may compete with those two lines.

## Design principles
- One screen. No navigation, no settings, no login.
- Motion is the content. Something visibly changes every 300 ms and the viewer
  should feel the rhythm of the clock.
- Everything shown is the run's own numbers, and the seed that produced them is
  on screen, so any of it can be reproduced.
- Legible in a compressed 1080p clip and at phone width. Big numbers, high
  contrast, no thin type.
- Restraint. Warm-neutral trading-desk aesthetic. Green = bid, red = offer,
  amber = stand-in. No gradients, no decorative charts.
- Honest about what is real. The market is simulated and the model is labelled a
  stand-in whenever no hosted model is connected; what is real is the decision,
  the order and the accounting. Every tick quotes, so spread capture and adverse
  selection are both visible, and losses are shown as plainly as gains.

## Layout (desktop 16:9 primary; mobile stacks vertically in the same order)

1. Header strip
   - Title, "tick 1,968" ticking every 300 ms, uptime.
   - A status pill reading `sim 300 ms` and `seed …`, because there is no wallet
     to show.
   - Model badge: the model's name, or `stand-in model` in amber.

2. Hero: price chart
   - ANTH/USD mid, rolling window of about 100 ticks.
   - A bead on every fill: green for a buy, red for a sell.
   - Current price in large type at the right edge, with the position and P&L.

3. Decision panel (the flicker; this is the signature element)
   - Updates every tick. Shows the decision for the current tick.
   - Two-way probability bar: buy vs sell, chosen side highlighted.
   - Decision latency in ms and the confidence for this tick.

4. Book ladder
   - The top of the book, offers above and bids below.
   - Our own resting quote shown as its own row, inside the spread, marked `us`.

5. Tape
   - One row per tick: tick, side, confidence, latency, what happened, order id.

6. Counters row
   - Last latency, average latency, decisions, fills, position, P&L, uptime.

## States
- Live: everything above.
- Model late: the tick cell goes amber, the decision panel shows "held".
- Stream disconnected: the header shows reconnecting and the chart freezes.

## Interactions (deliberately few)
- Hover a chart bead: tooltip with tick, price, and what we did.
- Nothing else. No trading controls for viewers.

## Non-goals
- No real money, no wallet, no keys, no chain.
- No comparison with other models. One model, one market.
- No user accounts, no chat, no text output from the model anywhere.
- No claim that any of this is a strategy that works.

## Technical constraints the design must respect
- 3.3 updates per second, indefinitely. Animations must be cheap: transforms and
  opacity only, no layout thrash.
- Constantly changing numbers need tabular numerals so tiles do not jitter.
- The whole simulation must be reproducible from `SEED`.

## Success criterion
A viewer with no context, watching a 20-second clip with the sound off,
understands in three seconds that a model is making a trade decision several
times a second, and can see what it costs.
